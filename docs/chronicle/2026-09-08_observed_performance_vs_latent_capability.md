# Chronicle — observed performance vs latent capability

**Date:** 2026-09-08  
**Status:** research-methodology extension; no validated MMALS claim changed

A review of Quattrociocchi, Capraro, and Marcus, *Statistical approximation is not general intelligence* (2026), triggered a broader methodological observation relevant to MMALS:

> Observed performance does not by itself identify the latent capability that produced it.

The strongest transferable point is not the ontological assertion that statistical approximation cannot constitute intelligence. Rather, it is the warning against promoting a narrow behavioral result into a broader latent-capability claim without additional evidence.

Applied to MMALS:

- task performance does not establish continual-learning competence;
- continual-learning benefit does not establish mutualism;
- routing success does not establish regime inference;
- useful embeddings do not establish sufficient predictive state;
- prediction does not establish a world model;
- benchmark passage is evidence for a claim, not the claim itself.

This led to a new qualification architecture in:

`docs/program/MMALS_QUALIFICATION_FRAMEWORK_2026-09-08.md`

The framework separates qualification into performance, continual adaptation, novelty/OOD, uncertainty, MMALS-specific structural claims, and future world-model-like predictive capability. It explicitly introduces a dedicated falsification program for the word **mutualistic**, including matched-capacity controls, no-exchange ablations, contribution interventions, cost accounting, and persistence across seeds/task orders/benchmark families.

No current RC2I conclusion is changed. The new framework is intended to structure later evidence and prevent terminology from outrunning demonstrated capability.
